About

Researchers have traditionally shown greater interest in documenting languages that are transmitted primarily through oral traditions, are gradually becoming endangered, or remain lesser known and under-documented. However, the use of even widely spoken and major languages, along with their associated cultural practices, may gradually decline due to the impacts of globalisation, language contact, socio-political factors, and several other underlying or yet unexplored reasons. Therefore, this archive aims to document not only lesser-known, undocumented, and endangered languages but also major and widely spoken languages whose use and associated cultural practices are undergoing gradual decline.

Northeastern India is a treasure trove of linguistic and cultural diversity. The region is home to languages belonging to several language families, including Tai-Kadai, Tibeto-Burman, Indo-Aryan, Austroasiatic (particularly the Munda languages), and Dravidian.

The Department of Linguistics at Gauhati University welcomes students from different states of Northeast India every year. This archive encourages each student to document their respective languages and cultures, thereby contributing to the preservation and documentation of the region's rich linguistic and cultural heritage.
